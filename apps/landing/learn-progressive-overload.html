Training

Progressive overload, without the spreadsheet

Why adding a little every week beats training hard at random — and what FynroX is doing with the sets you log.

6 min read · Updated 24 September 2026

The idea in one line

Muscle and strength adapt to a demand that keeps rising. If the demand stops rising, the adaptation stops with it. That is the whole principle; everything else is bookkeeping.

What "a little more" means

More is not only heavier. Over a week you can add load, add a rep at the same load, add a set, shorten the rest, or do the same work with better control. Any of those is progression. Chasing only the first one is how people stall at a weight they can move badly and not at all well.

A useful default for most lifts: keep the reps and add the smallest load increment your gym has when the last set felt like you had two reps in reserve. When it didn't, repeat the week. Repeating a week is progress too — it is how the next increase becomes possible.

Why intensity alone doesn't work

A session that leaves you flat on your back is easy to feel and hard to repeat. If you cannot repeat it on Thursday, the week's total work is lower than a duller plan would have produced. Training hard at random reliably produces two good sessions and five bad ones.

What FynroX does with your logs

Every set you log carries weight, reps, and RPE — how hard that set actually was. RPE is what turns a log into a decision: two people can lift the same weight for the same reps and only one of them has room to add next week.

The weekly check-in compares what you planned with what you logged and adjusts the next week from the gap, rather than assuming the plan happened. A week where you trained twice instead of four times does not get the same increase as a week where you trained four.

When not to add anything

Poor sleep, a jump in resting heart rate, or soreness that hasn't cleared are all reasons to repeat rather than progress. FynroX shows those trends next to your training for exactly this reason. Deloading on purpose is cheaper than deloading because you got injured.
